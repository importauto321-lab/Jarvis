Jarvis Privacy Policy

Last updated October 28, 2026

In short: Jarvis has no server and no account. Everything it saves stays in your own browser. It only sends data to the services you choose to use: Anthropic, to answer questions, and Google, for Gmail and Calendar. It has no ads, analytics or tracking.

Jarvis is a personal voice assistant web page hosted on GitHub Pages at importauto321-lab.github.io/Jarvis. This policy explains what the page stores, what it sends and to whom, and how to remove your data.

What Jarvis saves on your device

Jarvis saves the following in your browser's local storage on the device you're using. It's never uploaded to a Jarvis server, because there isn't one.

ItemWhy
Anthropic API keySo Jarvis can answer general questions using your own key
Google OAuth client IDSo Jarvis can ask Google for sign-in
Google sign-in (access token), that you connected Google, and your Google email addressSo you stay signed in until you tap Disconnect Google. Google makes each sign-in stop working after about an hour; Jarvis then renews it for the same account. Disconnect Google or Clear all keys removes all of this.
Notes you ask Jarvis to takeSo Jarvis can read them back to you
Voice, sound, listening and permission settingsSo your choices are remembered

The conversation and any email or calendar details Jarvis looks up are kept in memory only. They're gone when you close or reload the page, and they're never saved on your device.

What Jarvis sends, and to whom

  • Anthropic. When you ask something Jarvis can't answer on its own, it sends your question, the conversation so far, and your local time and time zone to Anthropic's API, using your API key. If Gmail and Calendar are connected, the email and calendar details Jarvis looks up to answer you are sent too. Anthropic's handling of this data is covered by its privacy policy and the terms of your API account.
  • Google (Gmail and Calendar). Only if you tap Connect Google. Jarvis then talks directly from your browser to Google's APIs to search and read your email, send email, and list and add calendar events. See Google's privacy policy.
  • Texts. When you ask Jarvis to text someone, it writes the message (with Claude, through Anthropic, unless you said the exact words) and opens your Messages app with it filled in. Jarvis never sends a text itself and never sees what happens in Messages; you tap Send there.
  • Speech. Listening and speaking use your browser's built-in speech features. Depending on your device and browser, Apple or Google may process the audio of what you say. Jarvis never receives or stores audio itself, only the words the browser recognizes.
  • Page resources. The page and all its code are served by GitHub Pages; GitHub can see your IP address and browser details, like any website host. Jarvis loads nothing from any other server, except Google's sign-in code when you connect Google. A strict Content Security Policy blocks the page from contacting any other site.

Jarvis doesn't sell, share or use your data for advertising, and it contains no analytics or tracking.

Google user data

When you connect Google, Jarvis asks only for read access:

  • gmail.readonly: to search and read your email when you ask about it
  • calendar.events.readonly: to list your events

Only if you turn on Let Jarvis send email and add calendar events in Settings does it also ask for:

  • gmail.send: to send an email, only after you confirm it
  • calendar.events: to add an event, only after you confirm it

Jarvis can't delete, forward, archive or change your email, and has no feature to change or delete calendar events. Jarvis uses this data only to answer your requests while you're using it. It's processed in your browser and isn't stored; only the sign-in described above is kept on your device. It's sent only to Anthropic, as described above, to answer your question. Jarvis's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. No person reads your Google data, and Jarvis doesn't use it for advertising or to train AI models.

Removing your data

  • In Jarvis, open Settings and tap Remove key and Disconnect.
  • To erase everything, clear this site's data in your browser. On iPad or iPhone, go to Settings → Apps → Safari → Advanced → Website Data (or Settings → Safari on older versions) and remove github.io.
  • You can also remove Jarvis's access to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Children

Jarvis isn't directed at children under 13.
